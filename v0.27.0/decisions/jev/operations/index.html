Decisions · Jev (TypeSafe)

Confidence, errors, cost & testing

Confidence and tuning

TypeSafe trains Jev for calibrated decisions: its probabilities are optimized against outcomes, and calibration is measured across groups of answers, so it does not guarantee that any single answer is correct (System One). TypeSafe computes the confidence of every Choice and Score answer from its probabilities, 1 when all of it is on one outcome and 0 when it is spread evenly, and a Noul answer carries none: its probability is the whole answer. TypeSafe's Confidence page gives the formulas of both; the Choice formula is also on Reading answers.

In the replies recorded for this manual, TypeSafe rounds every probability to two decimals, so a distribution need not sum to exactly 1: ask accepts a sum within 0.005 per outcome of 1, and a chosen option at most 0.01 below the most probable one, and returns a reply further off as a SystemOneCallError.

Answers vary between identical calls: a decision near a threshold can flip from one call to the next. Log what a decision was based on — the answer's raw, r.response.request_id and r.response.model — and record the answers a test relies on (Testing and recordings).

Evidence

A live reply over four options summed to 0.99 on 20 October 2026. Measured on jev-1.13.0 in September 2026, identical requests differed in 60% of repeated pairs, by 0.011 in a probability on average and by up to 0.11.

Jev answers every question it is asked: TypeSafe's answer types hold no refusal (System One), so a case is handed over only by a bar you set. Below min_confidence, a natural-language verb returns what fallback returns, and with no fallback it throws LowConfidenceError. A support desk's inbox, one team per message:

const TEAM = (billing   = "payments, charges, invoices or refunds",
              technical = "the app or the website does not work as expected",
              shipping  = "a parcel that is late, lost or arrived damaged",
              other     = "anything else")
const MESSAGES = [
    "I was charged twice for order #4471. Please refund the duplicate payment.",
    "The app crashes every time I open my order history. iPhone 15, latest version.",
    "My parcel was due last Monday and the tracking hasn't moved in six days.",
    "Do you ship to Norway, and how much does delivery cost?",
    "The phone I received has a cracked screen and the box was crushed. I want my money back.",
    "Your update deleted my saved addresses and now I can't check out. Third time this month!",
]

for message in MESSAGES
    println(rpad(nl_classify(message, TEAM), 11), message)
end

Every message gets a team. The question about shipping to Norway is the unclear one, and with a bar it goes to a person:

seen = SystemOneSuccess[]
team = nl_classify(MESSAGES[4], TEAM; min_confidence = 0.6, fallback = message -> :person,
                   on_response = r -> push!(seen, r))
a = only(seen)["classify"]
(team, findfirst(==(a.choice), TEAM), a.confidence)

Jev's best guess, billing, came with a confidence below the 0.6 asked for. Set the bar by what a wrong team costs against a person's time; where some mistakes cost more than others, price each one and let a decide policy choose the cheapest action (When mistakes have different prices). Tune a bar on messages of your own, with the version pinned: a bar tuned against one version is a claim about that version (Models). The measurements below were made on Jev 1.13, and on 25 October 2026 jev-latest points at Jev 1.14. The decision rules on every provider are on Act on the answer.

Evidence

Measured on 20 October 2026 on our 129 labelled support tickets, one Choice of seven intents per ticket, one call at a time, on jev-1.13.0: accuracy 0.969, expected calibration error 0.016 (10 bins), Brier score 0.052, mean top probability 0.96; 115 tickets at a confidence of 0.8 or more, of which 0.991 were right; latency 0.25 s at the median and 0.33 s at the 95th percentile. Limits: one labelled set, one run. In September 2026, also on jev-1.13.0, two other sets of ours measured higher errors: 0.047 on 100 items built on the model's documented failure modes, and 0.071 on 74 Noul answers, which were under-confident.

Cost

TypeSafe bills the input tokens of a request — the state, the questions and the pictures — and output tokens are free (Models, Images). The state is read once for every question of a request, so many questions in one ask cost far less than a request each. The rows are TypeSafe's prices as checked on 25 October 2026; prices change, so check the Models page before relying on an estimate.

message = MESSAGES[1]
r = ask(message, "team" => choice("Which team should handle this message?", TEAM))
u = token_usage(r)

 estimated_cost(r))

prompt_tokens is TypeSafe's input_tokens and completion_tokens its output_tokens. estimated_cost prices the versioned id that answered, r.response.model, not the alias the request named: here jev-1.14.0, which has a row of its own in the table above, so haskey is true. A jev-X.Y.Z id with no row of its own, such as a release newer than the table, is priced at the jev-latest row, and any other name with no row at 0.0, with a warning. What a picture costs is on Images; totals across calls and prices of your own are on Cost Tracking.

Testing and recordings

with_recorded_answers records a TypeSafe exchange once and replays it with no key and no network: ask, ask_each and the verbs built on them, and TypeSafe's list_models, the one model listing it records. A recording is filed under the request's own path, /v1/systemone or /v1/models, with no namespace in front of it, and keyed on the exact request: its method, its path and its body, which names the model. long_context = true is part of the key too; the pictures of a request are keyed by the SHA-256 of their bytes, and the file holds those digests, never the pictures.

recordings = mktempdir()      # in a package: a directory committed next to the tests
recorded = with_recorded_answers(recordings; mode = :record) do
    ask(message, "team" => choice("Which team should handle this message?", TEAM))
end
JSON.parsefile(only(readdir(recordings; join = true)))["request"]["path"]

replayed = withenv("TYPESAFE_API_KEY" => nothing) do
    with_recorded_answers(recordings) do
        ask(message, "team" => choice("Which team should handle this message?", TEAM))
    end
end
replayed["team"].probabilities == recorded["team"].probabilities

Recording takes TYPESAFE_API_KEY, and replaying takes nothing. In this manual's build the recording scope above replays the manual's own recordings, so the example reaches no service either (How this manual runs its examples).

  • Pin the model. A request's key holds the model it names. An unpinned request names the default model, an alias (at the top of the start page), so its recording keeps answering with the version it was recorded on after the alias moves; a pinned one misses once you move the pin, until it is recorded again. An unpinned request names TYPESAFE_DEFAULT_MODEL instead when that is set: record with the environment you replay with, or pin.
  • What a recording cannot hold. To test what your code does with an answer the model rarely gives — a near-tie, a failed call, an option it was never offered — point TYPESAFE_BASE_URL at a local server that answers what you wrote. Run such tests outside with_recorded_answers: in a replay scope the request is replayed or misses, and never reaches the server, and a recording scope would file the canned answers as real ones. This block is not run when the manual is built:
using UniLM, HTTP, JSON, Test

struct Email
    subject::String
    body::String
end
route(::nl"the customer wants a refund", msg)           = :refunds
route(::nl"the customer reports a bug in the app", msg) = :engineering
route(::nl"an automatic out-of-office reply", ::Email)  = :drop
route(::nl"anything else", msg)                         = :frontline

canned = Ref{Any}()                  # (status, body) the server answers next
sent = String[]                      # every request body it received
server = HTTP.serve!("127.0.0.1", 8123; verbose = false) do req
    push!(sent, String(copy(req.body)))
    status, body = canned[]
    HTTP.Response(status, ["Content-Type" => "application/json"], JSON.json(body))
end

const OPTIONS = meanings(route, Tuple{String})[1]     # what a String message is offered

# A 200 in the service's shape: route's one question is "meaning_1" (its slot is unnamed).
# `option` gets probability p, the others share the rest, and confidence is (n·p − 1)/(n − 1).
function choosing(option, p)
    n = length(OPTIONS)
    probabilities = Dict(o => o == option ? p : (1 - p) / (n - 1) for o in OPTIONS)
    (200, (model = "jev-1.13.0", usage = (input_tokens = 96, output_tokens = 3),
           answers = (meaning_1 = (type = "choice", choice = option, probabilities,
                                   confidence = clamp((n * p - 1) / (n - 1), 0, 1)),)))
end

withenv("TYPESAFE_BASE_URL" => "http://127.0.0.1:8123", "TYPESAFE_API_KEY" => "test") do
    @testset "route: resolution edge cases" begin
        canned[] = choosing("the customer wants a refund", 0.97)
        @test nl_dispatch(route, "I want my money back.") === :refunds
        wire = JSON.parse(last(sent))                     # the request, in wire order
        @test wire["state"] == Dict("msg" => "I want my money back.")
        @test collect(keys(wire["questions"]["meaning_1"]["criteria"])) == OPTIONS

        canned[] = choosing("the customer wants a refund", 0.40)        # confidence 0.1
        @test nl_dispatch(route, "Hm."; min_confidence = 0.7, fallback = m -> :human) === :human
        @test_throws LowConfidenceError nl_dispatch(route, "Hm."; min_confidence = 0.7)

        canned[] = (503, (detail = "Service overloaded",))
        @test_throws SystemOneError nl_dispatch(route, "Hm."; config = RequestConfig(max_attempts = 1))

        canned[] = choosing("an automatic out-of-office reply", 0.97)   # never offered for a String
        @test_throws SystemOneError nl_dispatch(route, "Hm.")
    end
end
close(server)

The response shape is the whole contract: a model, an answers object keyed by the question names the client sent, each a Choice with choice, confidence and probabilities, and a usage object. An answer outside the offered options makes the call a SystemOneCallError, so nl_dispatch throws a SystemOneError, and no method runs.

  • A pipeline with a language model. A test that also calls a language model records each request with its own provider's key and replays with none (Test a decision + LLM pipeline offline).

Previews of what a call sends, tests of the methods and of the judgment, and an audit trail are on Test and Develop.

Throughput

ask_each sends one request per item and returns the results in input order; on Jev it keeps at most 16 requests in flight unless its ntasks says otherwise. TypeSafe sets rate limits per organization, in tokens and in requests per second, shows them in its developer console, and answers a request over either with 429 (Models); higher limits come with its custom and enterprise plans. How a batch backs off when the service is busy is on Many requests at once.

results = ask_each(MESSAGES, "team" => choice("Which team should handle this message?", collect(TEAM)))
[(findfirst(==(r["team"].choice), TEAM), r["team"].confidence) for r in results]
Evidence

Measured live on 20 October 2026 (jev-1.13.0): 50 support tickets, three questions each, took 3.28 s through ask_each with its default of 16 in flight, and 13.21 s as 50 ask calls one after another. The batch used a single connection (HTTP/2).

When something fails

A call that reaches TypeSafe returns a SystemOneSuccess; a SystemOneFailure for a status other than 200, with the status, TypeSafe's message, its error_type where TypeSafe sends one, and the request_id; or a SystemOneCallError when no usable response came. A 200 whose answers do not fit the request — a question unanswered, an option that was not offered, probabilities that are no distribution within the rounding above — is a SystemOneCallError too: no success is built from a malformed answer. Reading an answer of a failed call (r["name"], answer, answers) throws a SystemOneError.

UniLM sends a request again after a 408, 429, 500, 502, 503, 504, 520 to 524 or 529, up to max_attempts times within total_deadline, waiting at least what the reply's retry-after-ms header asks for, else its Retry-After, as the shared backoff of ask_each does (Many requests at once). Each retry carries X-TypeSafe-Retry-Count, 1 on the second attempt. Any other status comes back as it is, since an identical request cannot fix it. With no RequestConfig of your own, a call to Jev is bounded by 60 s per attempt and 150 s in all; one of your own replaces both, per call or for a scope:

state     = "Help! My payouts have been failing for 3 days."
questions = ["urgency" => score("How urgent is this ticket?",
                                ["Can wait", "Needs attention this week", "Needs attention today"])]

r = ask(state, questions; config = RequestConfig(request_timeout = 20.0, max_attempts = 5))
scoped = with_request_config(request_timeout = 20.0, max_attempts = 1) do
    ask(state, questions)
end
(r["urgency"].score, issuccess(scoped))
